Why it lasts

Still looks like that a year from now

Most "custom" mugs are a sticker. A vinyl decal or a heat-transfer sheet pressed onto the outside — you can catch the edge of the print with a fingernail. A few months of hot water later, that edge lifts and your family slowly peels off in the sink.

We do it the other way. Dye sublimation on polymer-coated ceramic: under heat and pressure the ink skips straight past liquid, turns to gas, and settles inside the coating. When it cools, the picture is part of the surface. No edge to catch, nothing to lift, nothing to fade.

Run your thumb across a finished one. You'll feel glaze, not a sticker.

H3 Customs has been running presses in-house for years — tumblers, apparel, signage, one-off custom work. Same bench, same heat profiles, same color correction that keeps everybody's face from going orange. Memory Mug Company is that shop pointed at the thing people ask for most.

Questions

What people ask before they order

Will a photo off my phone actually look good?
Almost always, yes. Anything shot on a phone in roughly the last decade has plenty of resolution for an 8.5-inch print. Where it gets dicey is screenshots, photos that got squashed by a group text, and old scans. Send it anyway — a human looks at every file and tells you straight if it won't hold up, before you're charged.
How do I get my kid's drawing on there?
Lay it flat on the floor or the table in good daylight, hold your phone directly above it, take one picture. That's it. We clean up the shadows, straighten it out, and keep every wobbly crayon line exactly where your kid put it.
Does it really survive the dishwasher?
Yes. Because the image is cured into the coating instead of stuck on top of it, there's no edge for water to work under. Top rack, bottom rack, microwave — it holds.
How do I get you the photo?
Place the photo here so we can see the size and shape, then email it after you order. Straight off your phone is fine.
Can you take the background out?
Yep, and it's free. Very common with pets and with the one good photo of somebody that happens to have a stranger's elbow in it. We can also drop in a solid color, add a name, or combine two photos side by side. Just ask on the proof.
I need a pile of these by a specific date.
Tell us the date in your very first message and we'll tell you honestly whether we can hit it. Rush is often possible — ask before you order and we'll tell you honestly whether the date is doable.
What if I don't like the proof?
Then we change it. Crop, text, placement, a totally different photo — we'll keep adjusting it until you're happy. Nothing gets pressed until you say so.
Do you make anything besides mugs?
Mugs are the whole focus here — but the shop behind this does plenty more. H3 Customs runs custom tumblers in 20 oz and 30 oz, apparel, signage, and one-off fabrication. If you need something that isn't a mug, head over to h3customs.com and we'll take care of you.

Where do I find a variant ID?
  1. In Shopify admin, open Products and click the product.
  2. Under Variants, click the one you want.
  3. The browser address ends /variants/44012345678901 — that number.
  4. Make sure it is published to the Online Store channel, or the cart link comes up empty.

Setting up Uploadery

Uploadery is a paid Shopify app from ShopPad. It adds a real file field to a product page and saves the uploaded file onto the order, where you’ll find it on the order itself in Shopify admin.

  1. Install Uploadery from the Shopify App Store.
  2. In Uploadery, make an upload set with one file field — call it something like “Your photo” — and assign that set to the mug product.
  3. Open the mug on your storefront and copy the handle out of the address: /products/the-memory-mug.
  4. Paste it above, switch the setting to Uploaded with the order, and apply.
  5. Place one test order and check the file shows on it in Shopify admin before you send anyone here.

What changes. Orders hand over to the mug’s product page instead of straight to the cart, so the customer gets one extra click. A product URL carries no order note, so this site’s note box hides itself and points at Uploadery’s own fields instead. Some themes ignore the quantity in the link, so the page tells anyone ordering more than one to check it on the way through.
